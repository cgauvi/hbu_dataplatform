# Saguenay

The third city, and the first that is one partition rather than several. Since
2026-09-16 the pipeline scrapes Ville de Saguenay through the same assets as
Montreal and Quebec City, under a single key `SAG` covering the whole
municipality. This page is what is different about it: where each input comes
from, what the grid translation does with a by-law that states more margins
than the others, and which inputs are still another city's.

## The key, and why there is only one

`city_of("SAG")` is `City.SAGUENAY`. Saguenay has three arrondissements -
Chicoutimi, Jonquière and La Baie - and its administrative-limit layer draws
them, so a per-arrondissement axis was available and is deliberately not
taken. The city publishes **one** zoning layer, **one** road network and
**one** by-law (VS-R-2012-3) over the whole territory, and its zone numbers do
not encode the arrondissement the way Quebec City's leading digit does.
Splitting the city would put three partitions through the same city-wide
sources to no purpose.

What that costs is size: the bronze partition is a municipality of 2,839
zones rather than a borough of 800. The arrondissement and the secteur travel
as columns on the street network and are there for a reader who wants them;
nothing in the pipeline cuts on them. The lot chain does not run on the key
at all, though - it runs per cell of the tile cut, and a 1,150 km² city is
several cells at z9–z11 where a borough is one or two at z13–z14, which is
the point: the cell is about one run's worth of lots wherever it is. See
[Two spatial axes](architecture.md#two-spatial-axes). Saguenay's cells are
not in the cut until it is re-seeded over its cadastre (`scripts/seed_tile_cut.py`);
until then `neighborhood_cadastre` refuses to land it, naming the ground.

Both open-data snapshots are written *beside* the other cities' under the same
date partition: `reference_neighborhoods` writes `limites_saguenay.parquet`.
The streets are no longer written per city - `street_network` writes one
`street_segments.parquet` covering every city, cut out of the province-wide
RQTT. A snapshot taken before 2026-09-16 has no limits file, and a `SAG`
partition asked to cut against it fails naming `reference_neighborhoods`.

The metric CRS is **EPSG:32187** (NAD83 / MTM zone 7), shared with Quebec City
rather than borrowed from it: the zone runs 72°W–69°W on the 70°30′W meridian
and Saguenay spans 71°34′W–70°42′W, closer to the meridian than Quebec City
is.

## The grid is a document per zone, behind an id the polygons do not carry

This is the one awkward step, and it is worth understanding because it is the
slow part of a run.

The published PDF lives at
`https://zonage.saguenay.ca/rapports/v1/zonages/grille/pdf/<id>?diffusion=true`,
where `<id>` is the **zoning service's own primary key** and not `no_zone`:
zone 70520 is id 2700. The city's planning page resolves one from the other by
POSTing `no_zone` to
`https://ville.saguenay.ca/ajax/urbanisme/grilleusagesnormes_parnumerozone`, a
thin proxy in front of `api.saguenay.ca` - which answers 403 to everything
else. The proxy forwards **only** that one field: `page`, `per_page` and any
other parameter are ignored and the unfiltered first page of 500 comes back
instead. So the index cannot be pulled down in bulk, and one request per zone
is the honest cost of building it.

`neighborhood_features` pays that cost once, writes the resulting URL to the
zoning table as `LIEN_GRILLE` - the column name Montreal's zone table uses -
and everything downstream is then Montreal's path unchanged:

- `linked_documents` downloads one document per distinct link and flattens it;
- `zoning_grid_columns` parses it with `urban_rag.saguenay.parse_grid_pdf`;
- the corpus assets chunk, embed and index it.

That last point is the payoff. Quebec City publishes one workbook for the
whole city, so it has no per-zone document to index and `rag.chunks` has never
had a CIL row. Saguenay does, so its by-law is answerable in the chat from the
day the corpus runs.

## Reading the grid

`urban_rag.saguenay.parse_grid_pdf` reuses `urban_rag.zoning_grid`'s cell
machinery - a row is a run of positioned strings, a column is a cluster of
their midpoints - and differs from it in four ways that the published
documents force.

**The rows run up the page.** The reporting service lays its content out in an
inverted text space, so `_page_rows` hands back the amendment table first and
the letterhead last, on every grid. `_reading_order` decides from the section
numbers (1 to 13 down the page) rather than from the coordinate convention, so
the same grid typeset either way parses the same.

**The columns are tight.** A grid fits between one and forty-three columns
into the same band: nine columns sit twenty-four points apart, seventeen sit
eleven apart. Montreal's twelve-point tolerance would reach into the
neighbour, so cells are clustered at four points - one column's values agree
to within two across a whole page - and other cells are attributed within a
share of the measured pitch.

**A wide grid is several pages.** A zone authorising more classes than fit
across a sheet is printed as one sheet per slice of columns, each repeating
the row labels, interleaved with pages of the amendment table that carry no
grid. Zone 64500 takes three such sheets for its forty-one columns.
`column_index` is numbered across the document, because it is part of the key
`silver.zoning_grid_columns` is written on.

**Labels are matched unspaced.** These PDFs kern words apart a pair at a time,
so a row label arrives as `Latérale 1 (m ètre)` on one grid and
`Latérale 1(m ètre)` on the next. `_despace` removes all whitespace before
matching; it is also why `7 0520` and `70520` are the same zone.

### What is translated, and how faithfully

| Grid row | Field | Note |
|---|---|---|
| *1 - Classes d'usages permises* | `usages`, `usages_by_category` | The class code is printed *in* the column it heads. Emitted as the bare family letters `H`/`C`/`I`/`E` the solver's matchers read, with the by-law's own codes (`H01`, `c4a`) in `usages_by_category` - Saguenay's classes do not map onto Montreal's numbered ones |
| *4 - Structure du bâtiment principal* | `implantation_mode` | *Détachée (isolée)* → `I`, *Jumelée* → `J`, *En rangée* → `C`, in the `I-J-C` letters `postgis` already parses |
| *5-1 - Terrain: Largeur* | `min_lot_width_m` | The **lot's** width. *Largeur (mètre)* is printed again under 6-2 as the *building's* minimum width, which is not carried |
| *6-1 - Marges* | four margin fields | See below - this is the substantive difference |
| *6-2 - Hauteur (étage)* | `floors_min`, `floors_max` | Printed `min./max.` as `1/3` |
| *9/10 - prose* | `height_max_m` | *La hauteur totale maximale … est de 12,5 mètres* is a real ceiling stated in a sentence rather than a cell. Read, applied to every column of the zone, and the sentence carried in `notes` |
| *8 - Articles applicables* | `specific_articles` | Stated once for the zone |
| *2 / 3 - Usage spécifiquement autorisé / exclu* | `only_permitted_usages`, `excluded_usages` | The use is named in prose and the columns it applies to are starred |

**Not stated anywhere, and therefore not carried.** Section *6-3 - Rapports du
bâtiment principal* is blank on every grid sampled, so Saguenay states **no
site-coverage maximum and no density**: `site_coverage_max_pct` and
`density_max` are NULL, `coverage_cap_m2` is NULL, and `footprint_cap_binding`
reads `setbacks` on every row. The margins, not the coverage, decide what gets
built here.

**Dwelling ceilings come from the class name and go no further.** No grid
states a dwelling count in any row or note. `CLASS_MAX_DWELLINGS` reads
*unifamiliale*, *bifamiliale*, *trifamiliale* and *maison mobile* as 1, 2, 3
and 1, because the name fixes them; *multifamiliale catégorie A/B/C* and
*habitation collective* are ranges the definitions chapter of the by-law
carries and the grid never prints, so they are `None` - no ceiling - and a
column authorising one of them has no ceiling at all, which is the same rule
`program.class_max_dwellings` applies to Montreal's bare `H`. **If the by-law's
A/B/C ranges are ever transcribed, they belong in that mapping.**

**Levels are not stated either.** Saguenay's grid has no *Niveaux de bâtiment
autorisés* block: the by-law regulates which storeys a usage may occupy in its
own chapters. Every column therefore authorises its usages on every level,
which is what an unstated restriction means.

## The margins, and the one thing the lot decides

Saguenay's grid states **six** margins where Montreal states four and Quebec
City states three:

    Avant (mètre)                 the front
    Latérale 1 (mètre)            one side line
    Latérale 2 (mètre)            the other
    Latérale sur rue (mètre)      a side line that faces a street
    Arrière (mètre)               the rear
    Arrière sur rue (mètre)       a rear line that faces a street

`silver.lot_buildable_setbacks` sorts a parcel's boundary into four classes -
front, secondary (the rank-2 street edge), rear and side - and applies one
distance to each. Six figures have to become four, and two of the reductions
are decided when the grid is read while one is decided per lot.

**The two side margins become their mean.** The carve subtracts one distance
from every side-class edge, so 4 m on one side and 6 m on the other becomes
5 m on both. That is exact rather than a compromise: for a parcel whose side
lines are parallel - which is what a side line is - it removes precisely the
same ground, and it is the same argument the *jumelé* half-margin rests on.
Both printed figures survive in the column's `notes`.

**The not-on-street readings govern the rear and the sides.** A rear or side
line that *does* face a street was measured by `lot_frontage` as a street edge
and is in the secondary class, not the rear or side class - so `Arrière` and
`Latérale 1/2` are exactly the right figures for the classes that remain.

**Which on-street margin applies is a fact about the lot, not the zone.** The
secondary class is one thing here and two different lot lines out there: on a
corner lot the second street edge runs into the front and is a *side* line; on
a through lot it runs parallel to the front and is the *rear* line. A zone
contains both kinds of parcel, so the two figures cannot be collapsed when the
grid is read. Both travel on the envelope row -
`secondary_front_margin_min_m` and `rear_on_street_margin_min_m` (sql/012) -
and `compute_lot_buildable_setbacks` chooses per lot with the test it already
runs to tell a rear line from a side one: the rank-2 edge is segmentized and
each piece compared to the front edge, and the edge is *rear on street* when
more of its length comes back parallel than not.

`secondary_setback_rule` on `silver.lot_buildable_setbacks` records which was
read, exactly as `side_setback_rule` records the mode reading. It is
`secondary_front` on every Montreal and Quebec City row, because those grids
state one on-street margin and there is nothing to choose between.

## What a Saguenay partition does not carry

**No HMIP average rents.** Saguenay is a centre of the CMHC Rental Market
Survey and the vacancy workbook carries its eight quartiers, but the HMIP
reading-mode tool publishes no average-rent table for it: every geography id
from 1 to 3000 was walked in September 2026 and the tool serves 21
metropolitan areas, Saguenay not among them. `cmhc_rent_survey` skips the
centre with a warning and reports it under `centres_without_a_page`; nothing
fails. Saguenay dwellings are priced without a measured CMHC rent.

**No commercial rents of its own.** Cushman & Wakefield publishes no MarketBeat
for Saguenay, so `submarket_for("SAG")` is `None` and its lots are priced off
the Montreal whole-market row. Every such row reads `(Montreal proxy)` in its
`submarket` column and the asset's `priced_by_proxy_city` metadata is true -
the same treatment Quebec City gets.

**No construction costs of its own, and this one is unflagged.** The zef-builds
cost guide prices van/cal/edm/win/tor/ott/mtl/hal/stj and no Saguenay market,
so the Montreal column is used. Unlike the commercial rents, nothing on the row
says so.

**Zones the zoning service has never heard of.** The layer draws 2,837 distinct
zone numbers and the service lists 2,919 of its own; they do not agree exactly.
A polygon with no grid gets no envelope and no document, and the count comes
back as `num_zones_without_grid` on `neighborhood_features` rather than being
left to be noticed.

## Running it

Saguenay is one `make neighborhood-add` and then the same targets as any other
key - the script runs the borough steps once and each tile step once per cell
the city's lots fell in:

```sh
make neighborhood-add NEIGHBORHOOD=SAG
scripts/materialize_borough.sh SAG 2026-09-01
```

`make roll` now keeps three municipalities by default (`CODE_MUN` is
`["66023","23027","94068"]`); re-running it for a month rewrites the other two
cities' snapshots as well. `make comparables` wants Saguenay's own
*facteur comparatif*, which `rfu` reports as `saguenay_comparative_factor`.

Expect the grid index to be the slow step of the first run: 2,837 sequential
lookups at the resource's default 0.1 s pause is about five minutes of
waiting before a single PDF is fetched.
